How the calculator works Formulas & assumptions

Available agents = scheduled agent headcount − unavailable agents (shrinkage), with a minimum of zero. Shrinkage is a headcount, not a percentage. It is deducted from agents only; support call contribution is configured separately.

Daily call-handling time per available agent = shift seconds − daily ACW seconds, with a minimum of zero. ACW is the total wrap-up time for one available agent over the whole day, deducted once. The default 8.5-hour shift is 510 minutes. The ACW slider allows 60–150 minutes; the goal is marked in green at 105 minutes and current ACW in red at 120 minutes. Both are editable in Admin, with a warning above the current benchmark.

Calls per available agent = daily call-handling time ÷ AHT. AHT is talk and hold time per call, excluding ACW. AHT must be greater than zero. Click the green 4:46 goal or red 5:38 current benchmark to apply it; both values are editable in Admin.

Displayed daily average is calls per available agent. Headcount, reserve staffing, and shrinkage do not change this productivity figure; they change total team capacity. With zero available agents, the average still shows potential calls per available agent, while team capacity is zero.

Support / reserve HC adds agents who handle calls. Each reserve contributes 100% of an available agent’s capacity by default, using the same AHT and daily ACW. Use Admin to set a lower contribution for part-day support. Shrinkage is deducted from primary agents; reserve agents are additional available coverage.

Available calls on each weekday show the same daily team capacity. Covered demand is capped at incoming calls for that day; shortfall is any demand above capacity.

Team capacity = calls per available agent × (available agents + support × support contribution %). Fractional calls are retained until team capacity is rounded down. Support uses the same daily ACW and AHT when it contributes to calls.

Reverse planning checks staffing options and solves the selected variable—AHT or daily ACW—to meet the chosen day's demand and calls-per-available-agent target. The other productivity setting and unavailable agent headcount stay fixed. Staffing follows the admin support ratio. Rounding and slider limits may prevent an exact target; actual capacity and shortfall are always shown.

Capacity is not a response-time SLA. This model assumes demand can be handled throughout the shift. Arrival patterns, queues, occupancy buffer, abandonment, and interval staffing are not modeled. Daily ACW and unavailable agent headcount do not account for any additional breaks or meetings taken by available agents.

How Campaign performance works Inputs, linked productivity & estimates

Four linked controls. Choose combined Agent+Support headcount, AHT and daily ACW. Calls per available agent is calculated from (shift seconds − daily ACW) ÷ AHT. Editing calls per agent adjusts AHT while holding daily ACW fixed. AHT range and one-second rounding may limit the requested average. These are linked measures of productivity. With an 8.5-hour shift, 105 minutes ACW and 4 minutes 46 seconds AHT give approximately 85 calls per available agent per day.

Fixed staffing, predicted service. Campaign now keeps your selected headcount fixed and estimates outcomes. Service-level, wait and abandonment goals are comparison thresholds in Admin; changing a goal changes its met/missed indicator. They do not change predicted outcomes or automatically hire staff.

Shared settings. The Campaign Agent+Support Headcount slider has its own 0–175 range and scenario value; changing it leaves Agent view headcount unchanged. Until adjusted, it follows Agent plus Support headcount, capped at the Campaign maximum. AHT, daily ACW, weekday demand, shrinkage and support contribution are shared with Agent view. The selected total includes support once, retaining its configured contribution; support is allocated first up to the shared support count, with the remainder treated as primary agents. Available equivalents = max(0, headcount − unavailable agents) + support × contribution. Available call capacity uses the full productive time of those equivalents, rounded down to whole calls. All five weekdays use the same selected staffing; their incoming volumes can differ.

Queue model. An Erlang A model estimates outcomes from each day's incoming volume, AHT and available staffing. Productive staff hours are spread evenly over operating hours; concurrent agents are rounded down to whole agents as a conservative allowance. This can make queue-handled calls lower than theoretical available capacity. Daily ACW is deducted once from staff time, rather than added per call. The model assumes random arrivals at a constant daily rate, exponential service and caller patience, one first-come queue, no retries or transfers, and no separate occupancy ceiling.

Counts and service. Predicted handled and abandoned calls are rounded to whole calls and reconcile to queued calls. Handled calls are capped at available capacity; unused capacity does not guarantee every queued caller is answered. Average wait covers answered calls only; it is unavailable when there are no predicted answers. Service level is calls answered within the Admin threshold divided by all queued calls, including abandonments. Zero demand shows zero counts and no wait or service percentage.

Caller patience. The Admin value is an assumed mean patience for all callers, rather than a measured average among callers who abandoned. It is held fixed while you test staffing. Daily averages hide peak intervals; these results are planning scenarios rather than a forecast of an actual day's service.

Benchmarks. Blue lines are goals; red lines are current AHT, ACW and calls per agent. Click either a line or its label to apply it. Edit shared headcount and time benchmarks in Agent settings above Campaign configuration; edit the 85-call goal and 69.2-call current reference in Campaign settings. The goal combination is approximately 85 calls, 4:46 AHT and 105:00 daily ACW.

Reference model. Garnett, Mandelbaum & Reiman: Designing a Call Center with Impatient Customers (2002).
